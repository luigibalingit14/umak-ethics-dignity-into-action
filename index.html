Executive Research Brief

Dignity, Courage, into Action: Brief & Ethical Judgment

Scope: 800 – 1,000 Words

1. Context and Core Question

In the current student life at the University of Makati (UMak), digital spaces such as Messenger group chats, Discord servers, and unofficial confession pages serve as more than just communication channels. Often, they become breeding grounds for secret defamation, objectification, and cyberbullying. Within these "exclusive" group chats, intense peer pressure exists, where passive silence is considered agreement, and objection carries the threat of becoming an "outcast."

Focused Ethical Question: "What should a student do when pressured by friends in a group chat to participate, react, or forward defamatory memes and messages against a classmate? How can the virtues of Humility, Receptivity, Agency, and Courage (Fortitude) be used to combat cyberbullying?"

Campus Scenario: "Javier" (a UMak student) is in a 15-member friend group chat. One night, the group leader posts an embarrassing photo of their classmate "Leo" (secretly taken while crying over a failing grade), accompanied by an insulting meme. Everyone reacts with laughing emojis. The leader pressures them to share it with another section to make it viral. If Javier does not cooperate, he will be called a "killjoy" and could possibly be the group's next target.

2. Theoretical Framework (Analysis Using the Four Core Virtues)

This research is anchored in two primary books and divided into four core virtues:

A. From The Privilege of Being a Woman by Alice von Hildebrand:
Although this book focuses on the dignity of women, its philosophical message regarding respect for personhood is universal and directly addresses the problem of cyberbullying.

  • Humility: According to von Hildebrand, every person has sacred dignity and should never be used as an instrument for others' personal amusement or entertainment. In the context of cyberbullying, Humility is suppressing one's ego from seeking attention ("clout") or validation from peers at the expense of trampling on the victim's dignity. A humble person does not need to tear others down to feel superior.
  • Receptivity (Empathy): This is the ability to be sensitive and open to the feelings of others. In a digital world where it is easy to become numb behind a screen, Receptivity demands that we listen to and feel the severe psychological and emotional pain that online harassment causes the victim. This awakens our conscience.

B. From Boys to Men: The Transforming Power of Virtue by Tim Gray and Curtis Martin:
This emphasizes that true strength lies not in following the majority, but in doing what is right.

  • Agency (Individual Action and Responsibility): Agency is the understanding that you possess your own mind and moral responsibility. "Just going along with the group" or "going with the flow" is not a valid excuse. Agency opposes 'herd mentality' or blind peer conformity. You have the power to choose your actions, and therefore, you are accountable for every "haha" react or share you click.
  • Courage / Fortitude: According to Gray and Martin, virtue is a habit of moral strength. Fortitude is the courage to stand up for truth and justice, even at the risk of being abandoned, mocked, or removed by friends from the group. In a GC, courage is seen in outright refusing to bully and defending the oppressed.

3. Synthesis of the Books (Connection & Tension)

Synthesis of the Books: Both perspectives agree that a moral person is a defender of human dignity. Receptivity (von Hildebrand) provides us with the "moral vision" to see the victim's pain, while Agency and Courage (Gray & Martin) give us the strength to act and stop the bullying.

Receptivity sensitizes the moral conscience to emotional trauma behind the screen, while Agency and Fortitude prevent compassionate feeling from remaining inert by commanding decisive resistance against toxic peer pressure and herd conformity.

4. Contemporary Evidence and Legal Basis

Republic Act No. 11313 (Safe Spaces Act): Cyberbullying cannot be excused as "just a joke in the GC." Under Philippine law, particularly the provision on Gender-Based Online Sexual Harassment (GBOSH) and cyber harassment, the unauthorized capturing, meme creation, and spreading of defamatory photos online carry direct criminal liability. GC members who forward or collude may also be held liable (accomplice liability).

Counter-Objection (The Majority's Excuse): "No one will know because this is our private GC. It's better to stay quietly passive to avoid drama."

Ethical Response (The Group's Answer): Silence and indifference when someone is being abused in front of you (even digitally) is a form of "moral cowardice." According to moral philosophy, failing to stop evil when you have the capacity to do so makes you a "co-agent" or accomplice to the harm.

5. Ethical Action Plan & Recommendations

In response to the ethical question, here are the steps a UMak student should take based on the four virtues:

  1. Stop the Viral Momentum (Agency in Action): Use your freedom to refuse. Do not react (not even an emoji), do not save, and do not forward the meme. Cut the bullying supply chain.
  2. Private Empathy & Public Courage (Receptivity & Fortitude):
    (Private): Secretly message the victim (Leo) to let him know he has an ally and is not alone.
    (Public): Have the courage (Fortitude) to speak up in the GC: "Guys, this is too much. Let's delete this, we are destroying someone."
  3. Institutionalizing Digital Conduct (Humility): Establish "Safe Space Charters" within student organizations and class GCs at UMak. Remind everyone that true leadership and friendship are not based on belittling others (Humility) and strictly adhere to RA 11313.

Step 5 & 7 Practical Directives

Three Justified Practical Recommendations

Campus Behavioral Directives

Actionable interventions grounded in Christian virtue ethics and Philippine Law with explicit safeguards and real-world boundary limitations.

1

Stop the Viral Momentum (Agency in Action)

Use your freedom and moral responsibility to refuse. Do not react (not even an emoji), do not save, and do not forward the defamatory meme. Exercise personal agency to cut the bullying supply chain at your own screen.

Safeguard & Legal Protection: Shields the student from criminal accomplice liability under RA 11313 (Safe Spaces Act) and denies bullies the validation they seek. Limitation: Refusing to share halts your node, but does not erase the image from others' devices.
2

Private Empathy & Public Courage (Receptivity & Fortitude)

Private Empathy (Receptivity): Secretly message the victim (Leo) to let him know he has an ally and is not alone.
Public Courage (Fortitude): Have the courage to speak up in the GC: "Guys, this is too much. Let's delete this, we are destroying someone."

Safeguard & Moral Impact: Directly halts the bystander effect and restores the victim's psychological safety. Limitation: Requires fortitude against being labeled a "killjoy"; if harassment persists, escalate confidentially to UMak Student Affairs.
3

Institutionalizing Digital Conduct (Humility)

Establish and adopt "Safe Space Charters" within student organizations and class GCs at UMak. Remind everyone that true leadership and friendship are not based on belittling others (Humility), and strictly enforce adherence to RA 11313.

Safeguard & Cultural Shift: Transforms group chat norms from toxic clout-chasing into shared mutual respect for personhood. Limitation: Requires continuous faculty adviser support and proactive student council enforcement.
